# godot-3d-player-controller-v3

Godot 3D Player Controller v3 uses [CharacterBody3D](//docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html) and [AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationtree.html).

Click [here](https://timothycope.com/godot-3d-player-controller-v3/) to play!

---

## Features

- **Player controller** (`addons/3d_player_controller`): `CharacterBody3D` + `AnimationTree` locomotion state machine (standing, sprinting, crouching, jumping, climbing, hanging, swimming/diving, sliding, ragdoll, sitting), first/third-person camera with interaction prompts, equipment/combat, stamina, paraglider and skateboard gadgets, multiplayer synchronizer and voice chat.
- **Inventory** (`addons/garp`, Godot Action Role Play Inventory): equipment on the skeleton with the radial quick-select, BOTW style tabs of stacked items in a grid for pad, keyboard, mouse and touch, Zelda style Action pickups, and a `.tres` save in `user://`. The world player has `persist` on, so what you carry in `world.tscn` survives a restart. The details panel turns a 3D model of the focused item in place of its icon when the item (or equipment) has a `model_scene`, prints `description` flavour text for equipment too, and appends whatever the item's `get_details()` adds (a fish lists its lengths, the fishing rod says what bait is on the line and what it does).
- **Shooting**: the pistol and rifle are `Firearm` equipment that spawn physical `Projectile` rounds from a muzzle marker toward the camera's projectile ray, each with a muzzle flash under its `Muzzle` marker (`MuzzleFlash` wrapping a BinbunVFX flash scene, wired to the gun's `fired` signal in `world.tscn`: the pistol a half-size `short_flash_01` with a dim light, the rifle a `muzzle_flash_01` at near full size with a brighter one, both gone again in a fifth of a second), with a laser sight while aiming, and Gravity Sound's Gun SFX pack for their sounds: `resources/audio/pistol_shot.tres`, `rifle_shot.tres`, `pistol_reload.tres`, `rifle_reload.tres`, `gun_draw.tres` and `gun_holster.tres` are `AudioStreamRandomizer`s of three takes each (the clips converted to 96 kbps Vorbis, the WAVs of the used takes dropped), on the guns' `FireAudio` and `ReloadAudio` nodes and their `equip_sfx` / `stow_sfx` in `world.tscn`; a shot's sound travels with the round through the `ProjectileSpawner` (`Projectile.play_launch_sfx`, a speaker left at the muzzle), so every peer hears it where it was fired; the bow fires `Arrow` projectiles the same way. Every round sweeps a ray between physics steps, so fast shots reliably pop the red balloons and knock the beach ball around. Ammunition is an inventory item: `AmmoItem` resources under `resources/items/` (arrows, fire and ice arrows, pistol magazines, rifle clips and incendiary clips) are what the bow and the guns draw, one arrow per shot and one magazine per reload, and a gun with no magazine in the bag does not reload. Use on a stack selects that kind for its weapon (the grid marks the kind a weapon draws "Loaded", the plain fallback "Default"); with nothing selected regular arrows and plain clips are used, and a fire arrow or incendiary round flies as its own projectile scene, on every peer through the `ProjectileSpawner`. Elemental ammunition: `scenes/fire_arrow.tscn` (`FireArrow`) carries a scaled-down weather_fx `vfx_flame` on its head (both elemental arrows hang their effect on the `Tip` marker `arrow.tscn` now carries at the head end of the shaft, the end that leads in flight, the way a gun hangs its flash on `Muzzle`: the flame is an eighth of the weather_fx flame with its particles in local coordinates, so it rides the arrowhead in flight, on the string and where it sticks instead of trailing where it was emitted, and the ice arrow's frost sits on the same marker) and, where it lands, lights `GrassField` and `BurnableGrass` within 1.5 m through `Ability.ignite_grass` (no force flag, so rain still stops it), then burns on where it sticks for 3 s, and sets any enemy within that reach ablaze (see Spells); `scenes/incendiary_round.tscn` (`IncendiaryRound`) is the rifle bullet with an orange tracer and an ember trail that ignites and burns the same way; `scenes/ice_arrow.tscn` (`IceArrow`) trails a frost mist from its tip and, landing over water, is spent on an `IceBlock` instead of sticking. Only the round's multiplayer authority decides, and the `ProjectileSpawner` carries the result to every peer: `place()` spawns the slab, `ignite()` lights the grass over an RPC. Arrows leave on the crosshair line and fly a ballistic arc solved for the bow's speed and gravity, so they land where the crosshair points, at a target across the field or down on the pond; an ice arrow freezes the water where it broke the surface, and the bow shows the arrow it will fire next, a fire arrow burning or an ice arrow frosting on the string.
- **Abilities**: World of Warcraft style spells without a morphing action bar. Tap `Q` / Left Bumper to cast the picked ability, hold it for the ability wheel. Stealth turns the player into a pale ghost over a second (a depth pre-pass keeps limbs from showing through the body, a lesson from the old aethereal project) and makes the duck and Little Buddy lose them until an attack ends it; Heal is a 1.5 s cast that refills stamina and is interrupted by moving.
- **Vehicle**: a drivable Honda CR-V (`scenes/honda_crv.tscn`; walk up to it and its `PlayerDetection` sphere shows the prompt and turns the Action label into "Get In", GTA style, until you step away or press Action) with enter/exit animations and an in-car radio driven by the `radi_ot` addon (stations appear in the radial menu while driving). Its handling follows GTA V's model: a total drive force split across the driven wheels through a five-speed box, air drag that sets the top speed (about 150 km/h), one-g brakes with front bias, a traction curve that sheds grip once an axle slides past `traction_curve_lateral`, launch wheelspin, a handbrake that locks and unloads the rears for slides, anti-roll bars over soft suspension, and smoothed steering whose lock shrinks with speed and counter-steers into slides (`counter_steer_gain`). Every number is an export under "GTA Handling & Transmission" on the car. The chase camera sits `chase_distance` back (an export on the Driving state), ignores the car's own collision, and follows the direction of travel, so slides swing the view like GTA.
- **NPCs**: `FollowerNpc` companions that follow the player over the navigation mesh, swim in water areas and react to physics impacts: a duck that respawns as a giant, and a "little buddy" that can be picked up and thrown.
- **Fishing**: pick up the rod by the pool and press Action to cast the float where you aim (`FishingRod`, `Bobber`). A float that lands on dry ground just lies there until you reel in. When it lands on water the nearest fish shadow swims up beside it; nibbles dip the float with a small splash as the shadow bumps it, the bite plunges it with a big splash and a rumble as the shadow dives under, and a hooked fish drags the float about while you reel, and one press inside the hook window hooks the fish (the Action prompt reads Cast, Reel In and Hook! as the rod's state changes, and the Action button throbs green for as long as the hook window is open); reeling plays out on its own and the catch arcs into your hands; once it has landed there the `FishCaughtScreen` (`scenes/fish_caught_screen.tscn`, a child of the world player) holds it up Zelda style: the species' model turning in front of a fan of rays (`resources/shaders/radial_shine.gdshader`, a small CanvasItem shader of the project's own), its name, its length with a star when it is the record (Junk for junk) and its flavour text, the `description` on the `Fish`; Action, Confirm, Cancel or Start puts it away, and playing alone the world waits meanwhile. The rod's `CatchScreenTimer` (0.8 s) holds the screen back until the arc lands; a Player without the screen gets the HUD card (`FishCard`) as before. Each water's `Buoyancy` holds its fish table (`Fish` resources under `resources/fish/`, keyed by in-game hour, rain and lure) and `FishShadows` that swim under the surface; casting near a shadow or in the rain shortens the wait. A `Fish` is a GARP `Item` (`scenes/fish.gd` extends `Item`): the catch lands on the Food tab of the inventory (junk on Materials: the Old Boot, and the Crate of Boots, twelve of them in `resources/fish/boot_crate.tres`, whose `mass_kg` is at least eleven times a boot's while its `weight`, the bite chance, is a third of one; `mass_kg` on any junk prints under its conditions in the Fish Index), tinted with the species' `color` over the shared `fish.svg`, and each `.tres` also names its `model_scene`, hours, rain and `lures`. Lures are `Lure` items under `resources/lures/` (worm, dry fly, chum): use one from the inventory and it goes on the rod's line (`FishingRod.lure`, `lure_changed`), leaving the bag; every lure is consumable, so the bite eats it whether you land the fish or lose it (the bait's icon floats up off the float and fades as it goes, on every peer, `Bobber.show_bait_taken`), and the next one in the bag goes on by itself until the bag runs out and the hook is bare. Using a different bait puts the old one back in the bag. A bare hook is mostly junk: each `Fish` has a `bare_hook_chance` (0.05 by default, 1.0 on the boot and the crate) that scales its `weight` in the water's `pick_fish(lure)` and its `attract_range`, so real fish hardly ever take it and no shadow swims over; the crate of boots only turns up for a worm (`lures = [worm]`), the catfish only takes a worm, the trout only a fly, everything else bites on any bait. The rod's inventory entry says what is on the line, what the bait does ("Eaten with each bite"), what it tempts and how many are left, or "Bare hook: only junk bites", and redraws while the screen is open (`Equipment.details_changed`). Swimming up to a shadow (within its `scare_distance`, 1.5 m) scares it: it darts away from you, sinks out of sight and turns up elsewhere after `hide_seconds`. Sounds are exports on the rod, fish models are placeholders until real ones are assigned per `Fish`. A shadow only takes the bait when it is already within the species' `attract_range` (1 m by default, per `.tres`), so bites from further off come from fish you never saw; each `Fish` also lists the `biomes` it lives in, and the water's `biome_zone` (the `WeatherZone` it lies in, on `Buoyancy`) filters the table, with an empty list meaning any water. Casting and reeling are refused while a menu is open or from behind the wheel of the car (a horse or the boat is fine). A `Lure` also carries `bite_time_scale` and `attract_range_bonus`. Shoot a fish shadow and the fish is ruined but lands in your bag as `Chum` (`resources/lures/chum.tres`), which anything bites on, sooner and from further off. The `FishingLog` on the world player keeps every length still in the bag and the record per species (saved to `user://fishing_log.cfg`): a fish's inventory entry lists the lengths under its flavour text with a star on the record, the catch screen says when a catch is the record, and the pause menu's Fish Index (`scenes/fish_index_screen.tscn`, on the `Pause` node's `extra_screen_scene`) lists every species with its conditions from the `.tres`, a dark silhouette of the model until you have landed one, and the record size after; junk (the boot, the crate) is logged as found the first time it comes up, with "Found" in place of a record, and the index follows `FishingLog.changed` while it is open, so a catch shows up in it at once. The float spawns through the `ProjectileSpawner`, so other players see the float, its line (from the rod tip, or the left hand on peers without the rod), the dips and the catch.
- **Water**: the pool surface (`pond_water.gdshader` from WeatherFX) is a sum of six Gerstner waves fanned around the wind: the water travels sideways toward each crest as well as up, so the crests pinch sharp and the troughs lie broad, the shading comes from the displaced surface's own normal, and the most pinched crests (a collapsing Jacobian, as in [GodotOceanWaves](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotOceanWaves)) foam; `wave_amplitude`, `wave_frequency`, `wave_speed` (1 is physical deep-water dispersion), `wave_steepness`, `crest_foam` and `crest_light` tune it in `resources/pool_water_material.tres`. `Buoyancy.get_wave_displacement` mirrors the same wave table so the beach ball, the boat and the fishing float ride the real crests. Rain keeps landing as scattered drop rings on the surface. The pool's `WaterRipples` node (WeatherFX) simulates what the bodies do to the surface: anything in the water, swimmers, ducks, the fishing float and the boats, is rendered from above into a mask and pushes the water wherever it moves, so hulls leave hull-shaped wakes and bow waves, floats leave rings and strokes leave the shape of the strokes. Wire a water `Area3D`'s `body_entered` / `body_exited` to the node and point it at the water mesh.
- **Health**: everything that fights carries a `Health` node with a red health bar and a blue energy bar over its head (`StatusBars3D`); the Player ragdolls at zero and respawns after a few seconds. Bosses (`is_boss` on an enemy, or the giant duck) put their name and health on the Zelda-style boss bar at the top of the HUD. Kill the duck (or let it fall off the world) and it returns as the knife-wielding Giant Duck boss that hunts you; kill the giant and the duckling is back.
- **Spells**: the Player's ability wheel carries Stealth and seven World of Warcraft style spells re-created from the old `aethereal` project on the `Ability` system (`resources/abilities/*.tres`, tune everything in the inspector): **Firebolt** (`DamageAbility`, a homing fire bolt), **Fireball** (a bigger bolt that lights grass in a wider `element_radius` and sets its target ablaze, its damage over time), **Frostbolt** (hits, then slows the target to `slow_factor` of its speed for `slow_duration`; it carries Water, so it douses burning grass where it lands), **Lightning Bolt** (a 2 s cast, a near-instant bolt), **Flash of Light** (`HealAbility`, a 1.5 s heal on yourself or a locked-on Player), **Lightning** (`LightningAbility`, a `DamageAbility` with no bolt in flight: on impact it asks the weather's `LightningFX` for a sky strike on the target, so the spell and the thunderstorm share one lightning), **Chain Lightning** (`ChainLightningAbility`: hits the target, then jumps to the nearest unhit enemy within `jump_range` up to `max_jumps` times, the damage shrinking by `jump_damage_scale` each jump, every jump drawn as an arc by the same `LightningFX`; it never jumps to the caster or another Player), **Consecration** (`AreaDamageAbility`: instant, drops a `DamageZone` (`scenes/damage_zone.tscn`, an `Area3D` with a tick `Timer`) under you that hurts every body inside once a second for its `duration`, the caster aside) **Shadowstep** (`ShadowstepAbility`: instant, needs a target locked on or under the crosshair within `cast_range`, and puts you `behind_distance` behind its back facing it) and **Sword Slash** (`MeleeAbility`, `scenes/melee_ability.gd`: instant, no target needed; it hurts everything with `take_hit` within `reach` in the `arc_degrees` ahead of where you aim, with the `DamageAbility` slow and ticks per victim, and its swing is `scenes/sword_slash_vfx.tscn`, the Le Lu `Trails_VFX` fire sword on its showcase swing with a `GPUTrail3D` behind it, turned to face your aim at chest height at the size its scene authors; the sweep asks physics for bodies only, so zones, water, grass and pickups never crowd an enemy out of reach, and a swing that finds nothing plays no impact sound and lights nothing; the sounds are the TomMusic torch strike and impact until something better turns up). Anything that lights grass sets enemies ablaze too: `Ability.ignite_grass` (the fire arrow, the incendiary round, the fire spells) and the torch call `Ability.burn_around`, which tells every `Burnable` within reach to `burn(Ability.BURN_SECONDS, Ability.BURN_DAMAGE_PER_SECOND)`, 3 s at 5 a second; `EnemyNpc.burn` runs on the enemy's authority only, ticks the damage on its `BurnTickTimer` (every half second, straight to its health so it fights on without a flinch per tick), shows the weather_fx flame under `BurnVFX` and replicates `is_burning` (`resources/enemy_replication.tres`) so every peer sees it burn, and `extinguish` (the last tick, death, water: `Buoyancy` and a Water spell call it on anything that has it) puts it out. The Spells screen's details say what a spell does in numbers (`Ability.get_details`, shown in GARP's `DetailInfo` label): the hit, any `over_time_damage` ticks, the slow, and for fire "sets enemies ablaze: 15 damage over 3 s". A slow reaches its target through `slow(factor, seconds)`: the Player scales its movement wish by `movement_scale` (the blend walks where it would run), `FollowerNpc` / `EnemyNpc` scale the speed the navigation asks for, so their root motion picks the slower clip. Damage over time ticks through `take_hit` with the hit coming from the target itself, so the first hit's shove never repeats. Each spell's `cast_style` picks the cast clip for whatever the Player holds: sword-and-shield and greatsword casts channel in their own stance and land with their own cast or power-up clip, unarmed casts use the standing one-handed clips. Charge VFX ride the right hand and bolts leave from it. With nothing locked on, a bolt fires forward at whatever the crosshair points at, or just ahead; a lock makes it home. VFX come from the Le Lu packs under `Le_Lu/` (Fire, Elemental, Healing) and the Binbun packs under `assets/BinbunVFX*` (ice, magic orbs and projectiles, magic areas, smoke); sounds from `assets/tommusic/fantasy_sfx/Spells/`. The spell icons are the ones aethereal used, as SVGs from game-icons.net by Lorc (`fire-ray`, `fireball`, `ice-bolt`, `lightning-slashes`, `glowing-hands`, `fire-zone`, `cloak-dagger`, and `shadow-follower` for Shadowstep, which aethereal never had, under `addons/3d_player_controller/assets/game_icons/`), tinted per spell through `Ability.icon_color` on the wheel, the spell tree and the loadout. **Freeze** (`FreezeAbility`, `scenes/freeze_ability.gd`, `resources/abilities/freeze.tres`): a 1.5 s cast that freezes the water under the crosshair (or the locked-on target) into an `IceBlock` (`scenes/ice_block.tscn`: a walkable 2 x 0.6 x 2 m slab with its top just above the wave surface and half a metre of it below, deep enough for the swimmer's ledge ray (cast 0.3 m under the surface) to meet its flank, so a swimmer climbs out onto it the way they climb the pool's rim; it melts away after `lifetime`, 20 s, shrinking over its last `melt_seconds`); it is refused over dry ground, carries Water like Frostbolt, and sits behind Frostbolt on the QA tree. It shares the `ice-bolt` icon with Frostbolt in a whiter tint; its impact VFX is the Binbun ice area, its sounds TomMusic's Ice Freeze and Ice Wall. The aethereal shapeshifts (Bear, Treant and Ghost Wolf form) were not brought over: they swap the whole character model and need their own creatures. The Le Lu packs reference each other as `res://Le_Lu/<Pack>/...`, so every pack lives under `Le_Lu/` (the `Fire_VFX` trail script is renamed `FireVfxTrail3D` because Godot 4.8 ships a native `Trail3D`); Binbun scenes stay under `assets/`.
- **Melee**: Melee weapons carry a physical blade: an `AnimatableBody3D` named `WeaponBody` (the Hitbox's shape, on the `Weapons` physics layer 10, masking only `Hittable` layer 11, `sync_to_physics` off so it follows the hand bone) that `HitDetection` switches on only while a swing node plays, so a swing shoves the beach ball, the bowling pins, the torch, the plush and a felled log with the sword's real velocity and never touches the Player swinging it; swings apply no synthetic knockback any more.
- **Enemies**: four hostile mannequins east of the spawn (`EnemyNpc`, built on `FollowerNpc`): a swordsman, an archer, a rifleman and a spellcaster. Each idles until you strike it or step within five yards, then hunts you over the navmesh (a smoothed Idle/Walk/Run blend space like LittleBuddy's, moved by root motion as the Player is, so their feet never slide or freeze) and attacks in reach: a sword swing whose weapon hitbox has to touch you, arrows and bullets that need line of sight, or abilities (`NpcCaster`: Firebolt in range with line of sight, a self-heal below half health). Hits cost you health and shove you, while your melee hits on them only cost health and play their hit reaction (`ReactionHitOnRightSide`, `ReactionHitOnLeftSide`, `GettingHit`); a sword never sends an NPC flying; enough damage drops them into their ragdoll, and an arrow or bullet that lands on the head kills outright, for Players too: characters wear hurtboxes (an `Area3D` with a sphere on a `BoneAttachment3D`, physics layer 2, like a ragdoll's physical bones), the projectile sweep looks past the capsule for the hurtbox behind the impact, and the one named `Head` is a headshot. Add more parts the same way and read `Projectile.hit_part` in `register_projectile_hit`. Every shooter has a spread: an `Accuracy` resource per weapon (`addons/3d_player_controller/resources/accuracy/pistol|rifle|bow.tres`, tune the cone in the editor) that shrinks with the shooter's `skill_level`, on the Player and on each enemy scene (the archer and rifleman ship at level 4 with the same bow and rifle resources the Player's weapons use). When the Player they are hunting dies or gets more than `leash_distance` (30 m) from their post, a respawn included, they turn on any other living Player inside their aggro area or walk back to their post, stand as they stood and heal to full, like a leashed WoW mob. A Player in a vehicle is never chased: a car crossing the aggro sphere gets its driver hunted, they attack while it is inside their attack range, and they reset once it drives out of reach. Health, death and the animation state replicate from the server. The rifleman's rifle flashes at the muzzle on every shot, the same `MuzzleFlash` and BinbunVFX `muzzle_flash_01` the Player's rifle uses, wired to a new `EnemyNpc.fired` signal that the server relays to every peer by RPC so clients see the flash too.
- **Toon shading**: press `F6` to cycle Off, Newspaper, Cel, Binbun and BotW, or pick one under **Toon shading** in the Video settings. Newspaper is `ToonFilter`, a full-screen quad under the camera that flattens the picture into a few luminance bands and inks outlines where the depth buffer jumps, and works in every renderer. Cel is a `CelCompositorEffect` on the camera's compositor (Forward+ only, greyed out elsewhere and skipped by `F6`): three hard light bands with a mild saturation boost and bold ink from both depth and normal discontinuities, so creases inside a silhouette are outlined too. Binbun swaps every opaque standard material in the scene for Binbun's Ultimate Toon Shader (the addon carries a copy of `assets/BinbunMaterials/src/shader/stylized.gdshader`), each surface keeping its own texture and colour, so objects are lit in three hard steps with a tinted shadow and a light rim; water, grass, the sky and the VFX keep their own shaders. BotW swaps them the same way for the addon's Breath of the Wild shader (`addons/3d_player_controller/assets/shaders/botw_toon.gdshader`): two bands, the lit one in the sun's colour and the shadow one in a sky blue, a terminator that wobbles a little (animated noise in world space, BOTW's warbly shadow), a soft-edged highlight on the lit band and a rim that shows when a figure stands against the light, faintly on its lit side. It is written from what has been documented of the game's look and after NekotoArts' CC0 BoTW toon shader; the zeldaret decompilation is the game's C++ and holds no shader source to copy from. The choice is saved with the other video settings and is off by default.
- **Chat**: press Enter to open a World of Warcraft style chat window bottom-left (`ChatWindow`, movable and resizable); Enter or Send sends to every player over RPC, Escape cancels, and it fades out after 8 seconds until you hover it or a message arrives. Gameplay keys are blocked while typing. Lines starting with "/" are local commands: `/help` and `/teleport x y z`. With Steam loaded the Audio settings add a voice chat volume and a mute toggle.
- **Throwing**: with nothing in hand, hold `T` / Right Bumper to take a throwable out of the inventory into the throwing hand (the equipped weapon if it is `is_throwable`, else the item picked on the seeker wheel, else the first throwable carried), release to throw it with the Throw emote on the spine blend, so you keep running; a quick tap is a weak throw and the pause menu puts it back. Hold `I` / D-pad Up for the seeker wheel: ammunition kinds while aiming a bow or a gun, throwables otherwise; the HUD hint beside it reads Arrows or Ammo only while the weapon is aimed (focus held, or the string drawn) and Seeker the rest of the time, since a slung bow opens the throwables. What flies is a `ThrownItem` spawned through the `ProjectileSpawner`, so every peer sees it; where it first lands the server replaces it with a walk-over pickup (GARP's `item_pickup.tscn`, or the equipment's own scene) and anything with `take_hit` there takes the item's `throw_damage`.
- **Horse** (`scenes/horse.tscn`, `Horse`): a rideable on the player controller's `Riding` contract, stubbed for the N-Hance horse copied from aethereal (`assets/n_hance_studio/horse/`, the brown body and saddle only). Walk up, the prompt reads Mount, Action pins the Player to the `Seat` marker (the Riding state's `seat` contract) with hands off weapons, so the rider turns and moves with the horse in the same frame while the camera keeps its view; it steers like the Player on foot: the move input points somewhere relative to the camera and the horse turns to face it and walks that way, Sprint gallops at three times the walk, Jump hops, Action gets off at `DismountPoint`. The Player's own camera stays the view and still looks around, with the horse's body kept out of its spring arm. The clips are in place, not root motion (no position track moves over a cycle), so the body is driven by code and the scene's `AnimationTree` only follows: a `Locomotion` blend space read up its middle for the pace (`idle` at the origin, `walk` and `run` above it; the body turns to face the way it goes at the Player model's turn rate, so the `Walk_Left` / `Walk_Right`, `Run_Left` / `Run_Right` and `Walk_Backwards` cycles still in the space are not used) that `Horse.pace_blend()` eases toward, and a `JumpStart`, `JumpLoop`, `JumpEnd` chain whose transitions advance on the `is_jumping` flag and `is_on_floor()` through the tree's expression base node. The cycles loop through the FBX import settings (`Horse.fbx.import`), the jump start and end play once; `Swim` and friends, `Idle01`, `attack`, `hit`, `Stun` and `Death` are imported and waiting for states. The rider holds the seated `Driving` node (`rider_animation`) until a riding clip exists; the `Seat` marker is where the rider's feet go, 0.85 m up over the saddle, turned so its -Z is the horse's +Z heading and the rider sits facing the head with the camera looking ahead. It floats: in water (the world hands it the pool's `Buoyancy` through the same body signals as the swimmers) it settles with half its body under the surface (`swim_depth`) instead of sinking, the jump chain ends on the water as it would on the ground, paddles at `swim_speed` with no galloping or jumping, and with nobody on it turns and swims back to the last ground it stood on. The horse calls out on mount (TomMusic *Idle Horse 1*), on a jump and on dismount (*Idle Horse 2*) through `MountAudio`, `JumpAudio` and `DismountAudio` on `scenes/horse.tscn`, wired to its `mounted`, `jumped` and `dismounted` signals; the clips sit in `assets/tommusic/fantasy_sfx/OGG Files/SFX/Horse/Idle/`. The world bow's release plays the addon's *Bow Attack* set. A whistle (`K`, D-Pad Down, on foot) summons it: `Horse.summon_nearest` picks the closest horse within `summon_range` (60 m) and it comes over the navigation mesh through its `NavigationAgent3D`, galloping while far and walking the last stretch, stops `arrive_distance` (2.5 m) beside you facing you, emits `arrived` and calls out *Idle Horse 3* through `SummonAudio`; a second whistle while it is coming is ignored, another player's whistle retargets it, and mounting cancels it. The horse now replicates: `BodySynchronizer` (`resources/horse_replication.tres`) carries its transform, pace, jump flag and summon state from its authority, the server for the world horse and the rider's peer while ridden (handed over on mount and back on dismount, so other players see you ride), and a whistle on another peer reaches the authority through a `request_summon` RPC.
- **Pickups**: the weapons lying around the spawn and the fishing rod are walk-over pickups (`PlayerDetection` areas on each `Equipment` in `world.tscn`): the first time you walk over one you equip a copy and the pickup is spent, no prompt or button. This is the QA world, so a spawned player is also handed a kit through `STARTING_ITEMS` in `world.gd`: 10 worms, 2 rifle clips, 1 incendiary clip, 1 pistol magazine, 20 arrows, 5 fire arrows, 5 ice arrows, 5 rocks, 3 apples and a dagger stowed in the backpack (`resources/items/`), topped up to those counts on every spawn; the rock, the apple and the dagger are the throwables (a material that hurts, a food, a piece of equipment), the ammunition and lures are not. An item dropped from the inventory (or thrown and landed) lies there as its `model_scene` turning on the spot when it has one (the rock's, the fish models; the same model the inventory preview turns), its icon otherwise, and the "Pick Up" label on the Action button is up exactly while you stand in its reach: `ActionPrompt.show_for` keeps the label on `Player.prompt_action_label`, so the controls re-apply it through every other label refresh, and leaving, taking the item or the pickup vanishing (another peer took it) clears it. The guns reload from those clips and magazines and the bow shoots those arrows. Spells start at Stealth and Flash of Light with 6 skill points; the rest are on the QA spell tree (`resources/spells/qa_tree.tres`: Shadowstep behind Stealth, Consecration behind Flash of Light, Fireball and Frostbolt behind Firebolt, Freeze behind Frostbolt, Lightning Bolt behind both) on the Pause menu's Spells screen, where the Loadout page arranges the eight-slot ability wheel (`scenes/world_player.tscn` sets the tree and the points on the Player's `Spellbook`).
- **World interactions**: choppable trees and mineable ore (`Harvestable`, each strike fires `HitParticles` chips and the `hit_sfx` export through the replicated hit count, with `depleted_sfx` on the last one; placeholder chip particles live in `resources/vfx/`), a push button, a boat seat, warp zones, a kill zone that respawns the player, a moon with its own gravity, balloons to shoot, a bowling alley, and a pool with buoyancy (`Buoyancy` on the water area) that floats the beach ball and any other rigid body on the pond shader's waves while the boat rocks on them, its hull still masking the water with a stencil. The boat has heft: each axis is a critically damped spring easing toward the wave under the hull, so short chop is shrugged off and only the swell moves it; `response_time` on the boat (seconds, default 1) is how long the hull takes to settle, longer for a heavier, calmer boat.
- **Torch and fire**: a throwable torch (`Torch`) that ignites `weather_fx` grass fields and burnable grass. The fire spreads across the field as a ragged front leaning downwind, the blades glow, char and collapse to ash under it, and fires create thermal updrafts for the paraglider. Drop the torch in the pool and it goes out: `Buoyancy` calls `extinguish` on anything that has it as it enters the water, and its crackle loop goes with the flame (the scene leaves the loop's autoplay off and `Torch` re-applies the lit state on every tree entry, so picking an unlit torch up, which reparents it onto the spring arm, no longer restarts the sound). A lit torch thrown at an enemy sets it ablaze the way the fire arrow does.
- **Sky**: the world's `WeatherFX` is handed the `DirectionalLight3D` as its `sun_light`, so the sun turns with the clock the way the Date and Time demo's does (6h on the horizon, noon overhead, dimmed and blued at night, tinted per biome and blended between biomes at their borders) and the `WorldEnvironment` as its `world_environment` for the weather's fog. The sky is a Binbun stylized sky (`addons/weather_fx/assets/BinbunSky/skies/stylized/stylized_sky_01.tres`): a sky shader that reads the directional light for its own day, sunset and night colours and scrolls two layers of noise cloud, which is why it runs the same on Windows, macOS and the web export, where compositor effects and volumetric fog do not. `WeatherClouds` (`addons/weather_fx/scripts/weather_clouds.gd`) drives that shader from the weather: `cloud_density` and `cloud_color` per weather type (a thin bright scatter in blue sky, a grey sheet when cloudy, a dark one in rain, snow and storms) and `wind_speed` from the weather's wind, eased over `transition_seconds` so a change rolls in rather than snaps. It works on a copy of the sky material, so the Binbun asset on disk is never edited. At night the sky clouds dim: `WeatherClouds.night_sun` reads the sun's height and scales the cloud colour down to `night_dim` (0.22) with the sun below the horizon, eased through dusk, retargeting on every clock tick.
- **Retro computer**: a beige desktop on a desk by the spawn (`scenes/retro_computer.tscn`, `RetroComputer`) whose CRT is a 320x200 `SubViewport` behind a curved-glass shader (`scenes/crt_screen.gdshader`: bulge, scanlines, vignette and phosphor emission, so it glows at night). Look at it and press Action ("Use"): the view moves to the `ScreenCamera` in front of the tube, the Player is paused, the mouse is captured, every input is pushed into the viewport and the DOS prompt types `DOOM.EXE` and scrolls a startup log into the game. Escape / Start steps away and the screen drops back to the blinking `C:\>`. On Windows, macOS and in the web export the screen runs the real thing: `addons/pure_doom` is a GDExtension around [PureDOOM](https://github.com/Daivuk/PureDOOM), the single-header C port of the 1993 engine, built for x86_64 and as a WebAssembly side module; it plays the shareware `doom1.wad` straight into E1M1 with WASD (Space uses, letters stay free for the cheats, backquote opens the DOOM menu), mouse, pad (right stick turns, A uses, Back is the menu, the d-pad is automap, menu and weapon cycling) and sound effects, and stepping away freezes the game where it is. The music is DOOM's own MIDI, synthesised by `addons/midi` (Godot MIDI Player) through the SoundFont set in `soundfont` on the `Doom` node; leave it empty and the game is silent apart from effects. The on-screen controls, the crosshair and the layers listed in the computer's `hidden_while_in_use` (the world's clock and weather HUD) hide while you sit at the PC; touch players keep the controls. In their place the black bands either side of the monitor carry a plain-text controls card (`PureDoomControlsOverlay`, wording from `addons/pure_doom/resources/controls.tres`) that follows the input device: keyboard, Xbox, Nintendo or PlayStation names. Where that library is not built (`ClassDB.class_exists(&"PureDoom")` is false) the screen falls back to `scenes/doom.tscn`, `Doom`, an original raycaster in the spirit of the 1993 shooter with none of its assets: a 16x16 maze in the `MAP` constant (`#` stone, `B` brick, `T` tech panels, `P` start, `E` demon) drawn one screen column at a time, seven one-eyed demons that wake when they see you, hunt you over the grid and bite in reach, a pump shotgun that sways with your stride, and a status bar with ammo, health, kills and a face that glances about and grimaces when bitten. Walk with the move actions, turn with the mouse, a touch drag or the look stick, fire with Shoot (left click / right trigger); kill every demon for `LEVEL COMPLETE`, die and Fire restarts. The textures, sprites and shotgun are generated in code at start-up; `shoot_sfx`, `hurt_sfx` and `demon_death_sfx` on the `Doom` node are empty and wait for sounds. Other players see the screen but the game runs locally per peer. Drop the scene in any level; the screen is `local_to_scene` so several computers each get their own viewport. To play the raycaster by itself, open `scenes/doom_raycaster.tscn` and Run Current Scene: it fills the window with the 320x200 screen at 16:10, registers the addon's actions, captures the mouse (Escape lets go, a click takes it back without firing, and input only reaches the game while it is captured) and boots straight in with `use_engine` off on its `Doom` node, so it is the raycaster even where the PureDoom library is built.
- **Biomes blend**: the three `WeatherZone`s under `Zones` (plains, mountain, desert) overlap by 15 m, and `WeatherFX.blend_zones` turns that overlap into a gradient instead of a cut: with the local player as its `target_node` the weather weighs every zone by how deep inside its footprint the player stands (`WeatherZone.get_weight`, 1 from `blend_distance` = 8 m in, 0 at the side), makes the heaviest zone the biome and blends temperature, wind, sun colour and the grass and foliage tints toward the runner-up by their share, so the tundra's cold and blue-white light come on over the fifteen metres rather than at a line; the weather odds and the forecast stay the current biome's. A client reads its own biome off the zones around its own player while the rain stays the host's (`_sync_weather` only sets the biome on a peer with nothing to weigh). The addon's day and night ambience (`addons/weather_fx/scenes/bgs.tscn`, left in the addon) is instanced in `world.tscn` as `BackGroundSounds` and wired into `WeatherFX/WeatherAudio`'s `bgs_*` slots, so the forest loop follows the weather and the hour as in the addon's demo.
- **Weather, date and radio addons**: `weather_fx` (biomes, precipitation, wind, wildfire, and lightning: in a thunderstorm `LightningFX` under the WeatherFX node drops bolts around the player every few seconds with a flash and late thunder, hurting whatever stands under one and lighting the grass; its `strike_at` and `arc` are what the lightning spells draw with), `date_and_time` (clock and calendar HUD) and `radi_ot` (internet radio) work alongside each other and the player addon.
- **Steam multiplayer**: with GodotSteam present, the world auto-creates a public lobby and hosts it (`SteamPeer`); joining a lobby from the title screen connects to the owner. `PlayerSpawner` spawns `scenes/world_player.tscn` per peer (and previews the Player's model at the spawn point in the editor, so the map can be built around them), `ProjectileSpawner` replicates every bullet and arrow, the host owns the clock (`DateAndTime/TimeSynchronizer`), weather (relayed by RPC from `world.gd`), NPCs, physics props and harvestables (`BodySynchronizer`/`StateSynchronizer` in their scenes), and the car hands its authority to whoever drives it.

The spells, fish, lures and items a designer edits are `.tres` files, and they live at two levels:

- **Addon folders** (`addons/garp/resources/`, `addons/3d_player_controller/resources/`, `addons/radi_ot/resources/`) hold only examples for that addon's own demo and tests. A resource inside an addon must never point at `res://resources/...` or `res://scenes/...`: the GARP demo tree once referenced this project's Shadowstep and broke the addon on its own. ## Web export

The `Web` preset in `export_presets.cfg` exports only what the listed scenes reach (`export_filter="scenes"`) plus the include filter. Two rules keep that working. Anything a script reaches only by `preload`, `load` or a path in a String property (the pause menu's screens, the wind and lightning bolt scenes, spell projectiles) is not a scene dependency and has to be ticked in the export dialog, so it lands in `export_files`; tick those, not whole asset packs, since every ticked scene and all it reaches is exported (the VFX packs ticked wholesale made a 127 MB pack, over GitHub's 100 MB file limit; the referenced 79 scenes make 81 MB). The exporter takes dependencies from the editor's filesystem cache, `.godot/editor/filesystem_cache10`, not from the files; if a scene's files go missing from the pack although the scene is in it, delete that cache and export again. `tools/pck_report.py` on the exported pack shows what got in, and `tools/pck_missing.py` lists what it should carry but does not (a preload in a packed script, a scene a packed `.tres` names), which is how the arrow, rock, dagger, ice block and thrown item scenes and the bow's draw and put-away clips were found missing; a scene-filtered export walks only the scenes in `export_files`, so a listed `.tres` is copied but never walked, which is why the bow clips are `AudioStreamRandomizer` resources set on the bow pickup in `world.tscn` rather than script preloads. And the include filter lists `*.gdshaderinc` (shader include files are not resources; without them the BinbunVFX shaders fail in the browser with `#include` errors) and every image extension, because a material that comes in through the `*.tres` filter is not walked for its textures, which is how the horse lost its saddle. Exporting every image is cheap now that they are all Lossy at 512. Everything (`all_resources`) would be a 204 MB pack, mostly the audio and the VFX packs' demo scenes, so the scene list stays. GodotSteam has no wasm32 build and logs a warning at export; the code guards Steam behind `OS.has_feature("web")`.

The build is meant to stay under 100 MB (GitHub refuses a larger file). It is 84 MB now: 27 MB of textures, 19 MB of scenes (9 MB of it the Honda's mesh), 17 MB of audio and 7.5 MB of Doom. Audio is the second lever after textures: every looping ambience clip is a 60 s Vorbis stream, and the six weather_fx forest loops came in at about 500 kbps (4 MB each, 24 MB for the set) until they were re-encoded to 96 kbps (`ffmpeg -c:a libvorbis -q:a 2`, 0.8 MB each) along with the heavier rain and wind loops; keep new loops at that quality, since a `.ogg` goes into the pack byte for byte. `tools/web_smoke_test.py` serves `docs/` and drives the export in headless Chromium through Playwright (`pip install playwright && playwright install chromium`): click to start, Single-Player, the world, failing on any error the engine or the page logs, with screenshots and the console under `scratch/web_smoke/`. Textures are most of the rest. Every image import (`[importer_defaults]` in `project.godot`, and every existing `.import`) is Lossy (WebP), capped at 512 on its largest edge by `process/size_limit`, with `detect_3d/compress_to` off so a texture first seen in 3D is not switched back to VRAM compression; a PNG's own file size never reaches the pack, only what the importer makes of it. `tools/pck_report.py docs/index.pck` lists what fills an exported pack by extension, by source folder and by file, mapped back through the `.import` files. `tools/tinyify.py <folder> --engine local` downsizes the PNGs under a folder to 512 (the default, `--max-size` to change it) (Pillow only; `--engine tinypng` uses the TinyPNG API with `TINY_PNG_API_KEY` in `.env`) and stamps them with `TINYIFY_*` PNG metadata so they are skipped next time; the fish textures in `assets/cgtrader/remofair/Textures` are at 512 that way.

## Documentation

The general idea of this addon is that the [NodeStateMachine](/addons/3d_player_controller/scripts/state.gd) handles transitions between primary **Locomotion states** (e.g., "standing", "jumping", "climbing", "swimming"). Think of locomotion as what your character does with their lower body; only one locomotion state can be active at a time. Upper-body actions (like shooting or boxing) layer on top as secondary actions/sub-states.

The character's [AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationtree.html) controls the actual animation playback by reading boolean state flags on the Player (e.g., `is_sprinting`, `is_climbing`).

### Getting Started

1. Download `3d_player_controller-v3.#.#.zip` from the [Releases](https://github.com/kirbycope/godot-3d-player-controller-v3/releases) page
1. Copy the contents of the zip (the `3d-player-controller` folder) to your project's `addons` folder.
1. Drag-and-drop the `addons/3d_player_controller/scenes/player.tscn` file from the FileSystem dock into your scene.

### Adding (and Preparing) New Animations

1. Go to https://www.mixamo.com/#/ and log in
   - To download files, you must be logged in
1. Navigate to https://www.mixamo.com/#/?page=1&query=Y+Bot&type=Character and select the "Y Bot"
   - When prompted, select the "USE THIS CHARACTER" button
1. Navigate to https://www.mixamo.com/#/?page=1&query=Y+Bot&type=Motion%2CMotionPack
1. Search for your desired animation and select it
   - For example, search `Idle` and select the 5th result
1. Select the "DOWNLOAD" button
1. Change the "Skin" to "Without Skin" and then select the "DOWNLOAD" button
1. Move the downloaded file to `/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/animations/source/`
1. Open the project in a terminal
1. Run the following command: `blender --background --python tools/bake_root_motion.py`
   - This runs the [bake_root_motion](/tools/bake_root_motion.py) Blender/Python script to add a "root" bone and reparent the "hips" to it. The positional data is moved from "hips" to "root".
   - Once complete, the processed files are located in [/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/animations/root_motion/](/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/animations/root_motion/)
   - As an added benefit, the `.glb` files seem to be much smaller than the `.fbx` files.
1. Open the project in Godot
1. Select the new animation in the FileSystem (it will have the Scene icon)
1. Select the "Import" tab (near the top-left of the Editor, next to "Scene")
1. Change "Import As" to "Animation Library" and then select the "Reimport" button
1. Select the "Advanced..." button
1. Select "Skeleton3D" in the Scene tree
1. On the right side, under "Retarget > Bone Map", select "<empty>"
1. Select "Load..."
1. Find and select "C:/GitHub/godot-3d-player-controller-v3/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/characters/mixamo_root_bone_map.tres" and then select the "Open" button
1. On the left, select "AnimationPlayer > mixamo_com"
1. On the right, change "Settings > Loop Mode" to "Linear"
   - Do not do this step for one-shot animations, like emotes
1. Select the "Reimport" button
1. Open the [Player scene](/addons/3d_player_controller/scenes/player.tscn)
1. Select the "AnimationPlayer" from the Scene tree
1. Select "Animation > Manage Animations"
1. Select the "Load Library" button
1. Find and select your animation under [/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/animations/root_motion/](/addons/3d_player_controller/assets/mixamo/animations/root_motion/)
1. Select "Open"
1. Select "OK"
   - Your animation is now available to the AnimationPlayer and, by extension, the AnimationTree.

## GitHub Notes

### Releases

When code is merged into `main`, the GitHub Action at `.github/workflows/release-addon.yml` will:

1. Find the latest Git tag in the `vX.Y.Z` format
1. Increment the patch version (`Z`) by 1
1. Build `3d_player_controller-vX.Y.Z.zip` from `addons/3d_player_controller`
1. Publish a GitHub Release with the new tag and the zip as a downloadable asset

The first automated release starts from `v3.0.0` if no previous `v*` tag exists.
